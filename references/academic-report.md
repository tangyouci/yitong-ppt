# Academic report route

Use this route for paper presentations, direction tutorials, group meetings, defenses, seminars, and conference talks. Optimize for understanding and intellectual value, not visual spectacle.

## Define the learning outcome

Identify the audience's background, talk duration, report type, required prerequisites, and two or three durable takeaways. Aim for most of the intended audience to understand the problem, core idea, and evidence.

## Narrative patterns

### Paper presentation

`problem and relevance -> contribution in 2–3 sentences -> necessary background -> motivation and gap -> method overview -> module rationale -> experiments -> limitations -> reviewer view -> takeaways and follow-up`

Adapt the sequence to the paper and time limit. Do not force one slide per stage.

### Direction tutorial

`topic and relevance -> prerequisite concepts -> taxonomy or evolution -> representative mechanisms -> comparable trade-offs -> unresolved problems -> reading map and outlook`

Organize by idea or problem transition, not by a chronological list of papers.

### Own-research talk

`problem -> gap -> insight -> method -> evidence -> limitations -> contribution -> next question`

Separate completed results from proposed experiments.

## Useful page archetypes

- Problem scene with one precise failure or question.
- Related-work landscape or two-axis taxonomy.
- End-to-end method overview with readable inputs and outputs.
- Problem–mechanism–effect explanation for one module.
- Experiment question with comparison and interpretation.
- Ablation or diagnostic with what the evidence does and does not establish.
- Balanced reviewer assessment.
- Final page with two or three durable takeaways.

## Evidence checks

- Preserve terminology, equations, metric definitions, dataset splits, and evaluation protocol.
- Distinguish author claims, reported evidence, presenter interpretation, and proposed follow-up.
- Explain whether comparisons are fair and whether experiments test the stated motivation.
- Do not treat a numerically larger result as meaningful without considering magnitude, variance, task relevance, and protocol.
- Cite source figures and external facts close to their use.
