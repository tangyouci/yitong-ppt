# Shared design system

Apply these defaults unless the user provides an approved template or brand guide.

The repo's cat and its blue and amber eyes are a brand motif. They represent the evidence view and the audience view; they are not mandatory slide decorations or a required deck palette.

## Typography

- Use one Chinese family and one Latin family consistently. Prefer broadly available fonts such as Microsoft YaHei plus Aptos or Calibri; use a source-faithful serif font for academic quotations or citations when needed.
- Use about 30–36 pt for titles and 18–24 pt for body text on a 16:9 slide. Adjust for room size and content density.
- Keep body blocks short. Do not shrink text to rescue an overloaded slide; split the reasoning or replace prose with a diagram, comparison, or visual sequence.

## Canvas and hierarchy

- Default to 16:9 with stable margins, title alignment, navigation, and page numbering.
- Give each slide one main idea and one dominant visual hierarchy.
- Use section pages at meaningful transitions, not as decoration.
- Use whitespace to separate groups. Density should come from evidence and relationships, not from filling every gap.

## Color

- Default style: white or near-white background, black or near-black structure, and one accent color. The signature 异瞳 style uses red for the active claim, contradiction, key transition, or result delta.
- Add at most one restrained secondary color for stages or categories.
- Do not encode meaning by color alone. Maintain readable contrast.
- When an existing brand or master is supplied, follow it instead of forcing the default palette.

## Visual language

- Prefer real figures, experiment outputs, screenshots, field scenes, diagrams, timelines, and compact charts over generic icons.
- Place labels near the objects or transitions they explain.
- Use consistent connector weight and direction. Main flow should be visually stronger than secondary relations.
- Preserve image aspect ratio and crop intentionally.
- Use short clips or GIFs only when motion is essential, with a static fallback when possible.

## Density by route

- Academic: one reasoning step per slide, typically 45–60% meaningful visual area.
- Project: compact evidence and decision support, typically 55–70% meaningful visual area.

These are starting points, not quotas. Never invent content to fill space.

## Minimal visual QA

- No overflow, clipping, overlap, stretched media, broken glyphs, or unreadable labels.
- Comparable page types use consistent fonts, spacing, and hierarchy.
- Important claims have nearby evidence.
- Citations, metric scope, and status labels remain legible in the rendered slide.
- Decorative elements do not compete with the argument.
